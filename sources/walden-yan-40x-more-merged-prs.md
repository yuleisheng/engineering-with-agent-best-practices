# Source review: Walden Yan, *40x More Merged PRs With 2.7x the Engineers*

Walden Yan (co-founder and CPO, Cognition), AI Engineer CODE 2026, November 12, 2026, where the talk was listed as *A New Age of Software Engineering*. [Standalone video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc), published November 18, 2026. Timestamps refer to the standalone video. In the [Day 2 livestream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9JtCCZP2MQ&t=9210s), the same moments come 2:33:18 later.

**Evidence reviewed:**
- YouTube's transcript of the standalone video: all 104 caption segments.
- AI Engineer's [reading edition](https://ai.engineer/code/2026/talks/walden-yan): its editorial summary, the 217-passage livestream transcript, and its capture of the agent-testable codebase slide.
- The 12 chapter frames YouTube serves for the standalone video.

**What I couldn't verify:**
- YouTube's bot check blocked playback, so the slides were seen only in those low-resolution frames and the one capture. The 40x chart was not among them, and most small labels are unreadable.
- No full slide deck or standalone talk page was found.

**How to read the results:** Cognition sells Devin, and at [3:22–3:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=202s) Yan says Cognition helps large organizations adopt this way of working. Treat every result below as a single-source vendor report. The external references support or limit particular techniques. They don't confirm Cognition's internal setup or figures.

## Claim decisions

| Source claim or example | Verdict and reason | Bad fit if… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [0:38–1:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=38s): Over about a year, merged PRs grew 40x while engineering headcount grew 2.7x. Yan concludes each engineer is “more than 10x more productive” and the PRs are “just as high quality, if not higher.” | **Reject.** Merged PRs per engineer isn't a productivity measure, and the quality judgment isn't measured. The talk reports no reverts, incidents, or user outcomes; [Tan](lauren-tan-all-code-is-slop.md) at least gave a revert rate. AI Engineer's [reading edition](https://ai.engineer/code/2026/talks/walden-yan) calls the quality claim “the company's assessment.” [METR's trial](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) shows why self-assessment isn't enough. Developers expected AI to cut their task time by 24% and afterward believed it had cut it by 20%, while measured time rose 19%. METR's [2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) says developers are probably faster now, but its data are “only very weak evidence for the size of this increase.” [Practice](../practices/measure-outcomes.md). | PR size or splitting changed over the period, or failures are fixed forward without being counted. |
| [1:51–2:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=111s) and the [1:53 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=113s) (“Default autonomous; Human judgement where needed”): By default, PRs go from bug report to merge, monitoring, and maintenance “without a single human involved,” with escalations when judgment is needed. | **Reject.** Don't make unattended merge the default. The talk doesn't say what gates a merge, who reviews, or what triggers an escalation. [Tan](lauren-tan-all-code-is-slop.md) describes automerge too; [Pocock](matt-pocock-fixing-the-pr-bottleneck.md) keeps human review as the last layer. The catalog pilot keeps merge and deployment with humans. [Practice](../practices/validate-before-scaling.md). | Agent changes could reach users without trusted checks and approval, or no one has defined which decisions must escalate. |
| [2:14–3:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=134s): Engineers who only forward tickets to an agent won't keep up. Cognition split teams into pods of two or three engineers. Each pod also owns customer bug reports, QA, and product management, and sends implementation to agents. | **Defer.** This is an org-design claim with no comparison or outcome data, from a vendor that sells the change. It can't be tried in one repo. | Engineers lack customer access or product authority, or the work needs independent QA. |
| [3:31–4:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=211s): A customer's bug video is forwarded in Slack; Devin opens a PR and replies with a video of the fix, which Yan sends back to the customer. | **Keep with limits.** A reproduction in and a recording of the same scenario out make a useful before/after check, like [Tan's verification artifacts](lauren-tan-all-code-is-slop.md). A recording shows one path. [Practice](../practices/verify-real-behavior.md). | The fix changes data, permissions, or paths the recording doesn't show. |
| [4:16–4:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=256s): The “end goal” is to run the whole development lifecycle “without opening up an IDE or without opening up a single coding agent.” | **Reject.** Don't make skipping the diff a goal. [Tan](lauren-tan-all-code-is-slop.md) also argues for selective code reading. The catalog keeps code review for side effects and failure paths that a recording can't show. [Practice](../practices/verify-real-behavior.md). | Reviewers would approve on a recording alone when the change has side effects, permissions, or data changes. |
| [4:28–5:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=268s) and the [5:03 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=303s): Cognition used to test against staging and live services, with engineers clicking through flows by hand. Now it runs every service with Docker Compose and mocks every third-party service on one VM, so agents test without touching live services. “It's not just you purchase a coding agent.” | **Keep with limits.** Google's [larger-testing guidance](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch14.html) prefers hermetic, single-machine systems under test to shared staging. It advises against automated tests that call real third-party APIs, but warns that hermeticity and fidelity often conflict. [Twelve-Factor](https://12factor.net/dev-prod-parity) supports the Compose half: run the same type and version of each backing service in every environment. This is in tension with [Pocock's AudioContext example](matt-pocock-fixing-the-pr-bottleneck.md), where a fake can't reproduce real failures. Google's [test-doubles chapter](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch13.html) says fakes need their own tests against the real implementation. [Practice](../practices/provide-a-disposable-test-environment.md). | The risk is in third-party behavior the fakes don't reproduce, or no one keeps the fakes in step with the real services. |
| [5:17–5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=317s): The VMs are rebuilt from the latest code every hour, and Devin is the first responder when a rebuild fails. Each session started from Slack gets a fresh machine. | **Keep with limits.** The useful part is building the environment from main and treating a failed build as a broken build. Hourly is Cognition's cadence, not a finding. [Devin's docs](https://docs.devin.ai/onboard-devin/environment) describe sessions booting from a snapshot built from configuration. [Codespaces prebuilds](https://docs.github.com/en/codespaces/prebuilding-your-codespaces/about-github-codespaces-prebuilds) update on each push by default, so the pattern isn't tied to one agent. Review an agent's fix like any PR. [Practice](../practices/provide-a-disposable-test-environment.md). | Rebuilds cost more than the drift they catch, or a failing build could be “fixed” by skipping a setup step or check. |
| [5:46–6:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=346s): Devin writes and keeps updating its own skills for testing the codebase as the code changes. The [5:03 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=303s) labels this “Devins fix and maintain them.” | **Keep with limits.** Keep the instructions for running and checking a flow with the code, and update them when a run shows they're stale. This matches [Tan's verification-skill reference](lauren-tan-all-code-is-slop.md) and [Pocock's Retro](matt-pocock-fixing-the-pr-bottleneck.md). [Devin's docs](https://docs.devin.ai/product-guides/skills) say it suggests skill PRs after testing an app. Review changes to how something is checked like changes to a test's assertions. [ExecCritic](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09133v1) found that weak checks can misdirect repair. [Practice](../practices/verify-real-behavior.md). | The agent can change the expectations it is judged against without review. |
| [6:10–6:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=370s): Engineers no longer need laptops to test, and some work from iPads. Yan says cloud VMs are “much more secure” than developer machines that may have secrets “all over the place.” | **Defer.** The talk gives no threat model or incident data. Moving work to cloud VMs relocates credentials rather than removing them: [Devin's docs](https://docs.devin.ai/onboard-devin/environment) inject secrets as environment variables into builds and sessions. The catalog keeps a narrower point: test environments get no production credentials, consistent with [OWASP's advice](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/) to limit agent permissions to the minimum necessary. [Practice](../practices/provide-a-disposable-test-environment.md). | Images, snapshots, or sessions carry long-lived or production credentials, or more people and agents can reach them than could reach laptops. |
| [6:40–7:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=400s) and the [6:39 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=399s): To test Devin joining Zoom calls, Devin created several accounts that joined one call from a single VM and exchanged voice memos. | **Keep with limits.** A self-contained environment can drive multi-user flows that are tedious to click through. The talk doesn't say what the test asserted, or whether the call used Zoom's live service, which bears on the “no live services” claim. [Practice](../practices/provide-a-disposable-test-environment.md). | The behavior depends on network conditions or provider behavior the setup doesn't reproduce. |
| [7:05–8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=425s) and the [7:31 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=451s) (“Recursive Self-Improvement for Agents”): Agents analyze tracked runs for wasted time and tokens and post ideas every day. Examples: a Node setup change to “shave like a minute” off CI, or a lint change to catch “20% more” bugs before AI code review. | **Keep with limits.** The figures are example suggestions, not reported results. Turning repeat findings into checks matches [Pocock's Retro](matt-pocock-fixing-the-pr-bottleneck.md) and [Tan's issue-feedback slide](lauren-tan-all-code-is-slop.md). Yan mines all runs rather than one session. “Before they ever hit AI code review” implies the same order as Pocock's layers: checks first, then automated review. Verify each claimed gain before adopting it. [Practice](../practices/enforce-repeatable-rules.md). | No one checks a suggestion before applying it, or analyzing runs would expose sensitive data. |
| [8:03–9:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=483s) and the [8:16 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=496s): “Jobs, not tasks.” Asked to improve P99 latency, an agent “should” add telemetry and alerts, break down the work, and send weekly and daily reports. Cognition wants engineers giving goals, not prompts. | **Keep with limits.** The P99 example is what Yan says development “should look like,” not a reported result. The useful part is defining a measured target, workload, guardrails, and reporting cadence before delegating. “Not prompting most of the time” goes further than Tan's advice to strengthen one loop before scaling, so start with the [pilot](../practices/validate-before-scaling.md). Telemetry and alert changes still go through review. [Practice](../practices/delegate-against-a-fixed-metric.md). | The target can't be computed automatically from a reproducible workload, or the job needs production write access. |
| [9:15–9:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=555s): “Find a way to measure” anything that seems unmeasurable, such as click latency, layout shifts from element bounding boxes, or onboarding retention. Then “set up an agent to go optimize that number 24-7.” | **Reject.** Don't make round-the-clock optimization of a single number the default. Click latency and layout shift have established measures: [INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp), and [CLS](https://web.dev/articles/cls), which scores how visible elements move between frames. Retention is slow, confounded, and moved by user-facing choices. Google Cloud's [AlphaEvolve guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/alphaevolve/developer-guide/quantify-optimization-target) requires a score computed without human judgment, and warns that a raw LLM rubric is “quick to reward-hack.” Sakana's AI CUDA Engineer [found a memory exploit](https://x.com/SakanaAILabs/status/1892992938013270019) in its evaluation code that, in some cases, let it skip the correctness check. Re-evaluated on Sakana's hardened robust-kbench, the reported mean speedup fell from 3.13x to 1.49x ([update, in Japanese](https://sakana.ai/ai-cuda-engineer-update/)). [Practice](../practices/delegate-against-a-fixed-metric.md). | The number can improve while what users care about gets worse, or the agent can influence how the number is computed. |
| [9:49–11:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=589s) and the [9:53 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=593s) (“2.8x TPM on NVIDIA Vera Rubins”): Cognition gave an agent dedicated GPUs and a target of output tokens per minute. Over about a week it found “eight or nine” changes, and a human engineer gave final approval. Yan says throughput went from worse than GB300s to “significantly better,” all “by just giving an end goal” ([11:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=661s)). | **Keep with limits.** The reported setup is the practice: reserved hardware, one metric, and human approval. That is more than “just giving an end goal.” The talk doesn't say how output correctness was checked, how many runs back the figure, or how strong the untuned starting point on new hardware was. Google DeepMind's [AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) independently reports evaluator-driven kernel gains, with every candidate verified and scored by automated evaluators. Its results are also self-reported. [Practice](../practices/delegate-against-a-fixed-metric.md). | The benchmark can be satisfied by skipping work, or gains on it don't carry to production traffic. |
| [11:08–12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=668s): One engineer gave Devin an ongoing job: keep an Android app at parity with the team's iOS app. Hooks watch the iOS codebase and Slack feedback, and Devin runs live QA. At [12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=765s), the QA is against iOS “pixel for pixel.” | **Keep with limits.** Using an existing implementation as the oracle is [differential testing](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/bill-mckeeman/DifferentailTesting.pdf). McKeeman notes that two correct programs can still differ, so list the intended differences, such as platform conventions. [Practice](../practices/verify-real-behavior.md). | The reference implementation is itself wrong, or the platforms should behave differently. |
| [12:20–13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=740s) and the [13:16 slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=796s): Over 44 days, “most days” had no human message, and on those days the agent merged “40, 50 PRs a day.” The app is “not a slop application” but not yet released. The slide counts 183 messages, 1,961 words, and 22 days without a message: “Six pull requests merged for every message he sent.” | **Reject.** PRs per human message measures autonomy, not quality, and the app hadn't shipped. The slide counts 22 days without a message over the 44 days Yan cites: half, not “most.” The talk doesn't say who reviewed 40–50 merges a day, the bottleneck [Pocock](matt-pocock-fixing-the-pr-bottleneck.md) addresses. [Practice](../practices/measure-outcomes.md). | Fewer human messages would be read as success before the app is checked against users or its reference. |
| [13:34–13:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=814s): With the loops set up correctly, “most teams are limited by the amount of ambition they have.” | **Reject.** At [4:28–4:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U92J5mCDLMc&t=268s), Yan himself calls the infrastructure “the hard part.” Each reported example depended on an environment, a metric, or an approval step. | A team expands agent autonomy before it has a working environment, checks, and review. |
